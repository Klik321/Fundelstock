# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Fundamental stock traders and serious retail investors who make decisions on
company and macro fundamentals, not just price action. They arrive wanting to
answer one question fast: *"What's moving the market right now, and which
sectors or indices does it touch?"* They are comfortable with dense financial
interfaces (they live in TradingView, Bloomberg, brokerage terminals) and they
judge a tool's credibility in seconds by how professional and trustworthy it
looks. They scan more than they read: headlines, sentiment, sector tags, charts.
Context is often a second monitor during market hours, plus quick mobile checks.

## Product Purpose

Fundelstock aggregates live, market-moving financial news and organizes it the
way a fundamental trader thinks: by GICS sector and by global index, with
sentiment tagging, breaking-news surfacing, and embedded live charts. It exists
to collapse the gap between "news broke" and "I understand which part of my
universe it affects." Success looks like a trader treating it as a credible,
glanceable command center — fast to load, never showing fake or empty data, and
premium enough that it earns a pinned tab next to their broker.

## Brand Personality

Three words: **authoritative, precise, premium.** The voice is that of a calm,
well-informed desk analyst — confident and data-literate, never hype-y or
"crypto-bro." It should evoke the trust of an institutional terminal married to
the craft and depth of high-end consumer software. Quietly impressive: the
polish is felt, not shouted. Numbers and charts are the heroes; chrome serves
the data.

## Anti-references

- **Generic "dark SaaS template"** — the indistinct purple/indigo gradient
  hero, neon glow, and floating-blob background that signals AI-generated
  landing page. Fundelstock is dark because traders work dark, not for fashion.
- **Crypto-bro / meme-coin dashboards** — garish neon, hype copy, rocket
  emojis as a design language, aggressive overstimulation.
- **Muddy glassmorphism** — washed-out frosted panels with low contrast and no
  clear edges. Our "Liquid Glass" must read as crisp depth, not fog.
- **Decorative gradient text** on headings and metrics — a common AI tell.
  Numbers stay solid, legible, and tabular.
- **Fake-data theater** — placeholder "0%" / "0" values, lorem-ipsus charts, or
  empty sections with no honest loading/empty state. Misleading data is worse
  than none.

## Design Principles

1. **Data is the hero, chrome is the frame.** Every visual decision should make
   numbers, headlines, and charts easier to read and trust. Ornament that
   competes with data loses.
2. **Never lie with zeros.** A value is real, loading, or honestly empty —
   never a misleading placeholder. Trust is the product.
3. **Premium through restraint.** Depth, blur, and motion are used sparingly and
   precisely so they read as craft, not noise. If an effect doesn't add clarity
   or delight, it's cut.
4. **Glanceability over density-for-its-own-sake.** Traders scan. Hierarchy,
   sentiment color, and sector grouping should let the eye land on "what
   matters now" in under two seconds.
5. **Respect the professional.** No hand-holding, no hype, no dark patterns.
   Treat the user as an expert and the interface as a tool they rely on.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.1 AA. Dark UI must maintain ≥4.5:1 contrast for body text and
≥3:1 for large text/UI — tertiary gray text on dark surfaces is a known risk to
audit. Sentiment must never be conveyed by color alone (pair green/red with
labels/icons for color-blind users). Honor `prefers-reduced-motion` across all
count-ups, reveals, and micro-interactions. Full keyboard navigation with
visible focus states; semantic landmarks; alt text on article imagery; ARIA
labels on icon-only controls (search, theme, watchlist, chat).
